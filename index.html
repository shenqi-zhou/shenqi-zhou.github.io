Hi, I’m Shenqi. I’m a final-year undergraduate in Biomedical Engineering at Zhejiang University. Since May 2026, I’ve also been a research intern at the National University of Singapore, working with Prof. Dianbo Liu. I’m interested in AI for healthcare and in how we evaluate LLMs and AI agents.

My current project, Auto-MD-bench, is a benchmark for AI agents that build medical prediction models on their own. These agents are usually guided by a single score, such as AUROC. The benchmark also checks whether the models give well-calibrated risk estimates, and whether they hold up on patients from other cohorts, hospitals or time periods. Before that, I worked with Prof. Nai Ding at Zhejiang University, comparing how people and LLMs understand Chinese sentences.

Manuscript

Auto-MD-bench: Evaluating Autoresearch Agents for Real-World Medical Tasks

Shenqi Zhou*, Qiran Zou*, Wenhao Zhao, Zhenyu Liao, Dianbo Liu

Under review. *Equal contribution.

We built a benchmark of 15 medical prediction tasks from 10 clinical datasets and used it to evaluate four AI agents. The strongest agents reach competitive scores on the primary metric, but their predicted risks are less calibrated than those of simple baseline models. Models with nearly the same primary score can differ markedly in calibration, and performance varies substantially when the patient population shifts. So the primary score alone is not enough to judge these models for medical use.

Research

Auto-MD-bench

Aug 2026 – present

Research intern at the National University of Singapore, advised by Prof. Dianbo Liu

I led the design and implementation of the benchmark, ran all the experiments and analyses, and wrote the manuscript. I chose the 10 clinical datasets and defined the 15 tasks (survival analysis, classification and sequential prediction), five of which are external validation tasks. All four agents had the same data, language model and compute budget, and I compared them on predictive performance, calibration and generalization.

Because higher predictive scores did not guarantee well-calibrated risk estimates, I analyzed the agents’ code and development records and found modeling practices associated with this gap. In one follow-up experiment, changing the agents’ optimization objective improved calibration while predictive performance stayed about the same.

Evaluating Chinese sentence understanding in large language models

Apr 2025 – Mar 2026

Undergraduate researcher at Zhejiang University, advised by Prof. Nai Ding

I studied how people and LLMs judge pairs of Chinese sentences: how related they are in meaning, and whether the first implies the second (entailment direction). I built 370 sentences from 50 premises. Three LLMs (DeepSeek V3.1, Gemini 2.5 Flash and GPT-4.1 Mini) judged ordered pairs of these sentences, and I collected human judgments on 2,577 selected pairs.

I adapted Sentence-Space Metrics to compare the two sets of judgments. The models agreed with people more on relatedness than on entailment direction, which suggests a gap between recognizing shared content and judging which way an inference goes.

Course projects

Skin lesion classification on HAM10000

Spring 2026

Biomedical Image Processing course, Zhejiang University · Lead of a five-person team

Our team built traditional models on hand-crafted features and a deep EfficientNetV2 ensemble to classify dermoscopy images into seven types of skin lesions. My own part was the evaluation and error analysis: because the classes are very imbalanced, I reported balanced accuracy and macro F1 alongside accuracy, and broke the errors down by class and body site. About a third of the deep model’s errors (24 of 74) were melanomas predicted as benign nevi, so we recommended human review and model calibration for such cases.
